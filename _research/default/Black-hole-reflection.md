    <h4 class="card-title">Black Hole Reflection Modeling</h4>
    <hr />
    <p class="card-text">
      Astrophysical black hole can be described with only two parameters: mass and spin. Spin, in particular, plays a key role in describing the dynamics of accretion onto the black hole. For rapidly spinning black holes, matter can sometimes spiral in towards very tight orbits, wherein relativistic effects strongly distort the light we observe. By applying physical models that account for both the structure and composition of the accretion disk plus the warped spacetime the light travels through, we can test parameters of the physical system to explore the black hole's key attributes and the dynamics of accretion in such an extreme environment.
    </p>
    <p>
      One potential problem with actually capturing data to which these models can be applied is the extreme brightness of black hole X-ray binaries in outburst. Even for a telescope which is specifically designed to accommodate very high fluxes of photons, like NuSTAR, there are limits to how much data can be stored onboard the satellite before downlinking to Earth (physically limited by the telescope's orbit past ground stations). For sources undergoing extremely bright outbursts, stray light can be used to alleviate some of the telemetry load, allowing for longer observations at the source's brightest states.  
    </p>
    <p>
      Using stray light observations of MAXI J1535-571, I am showing that this stray light data can be used to produce meaningful constraints on the black hole's spin, inclination, and the dynamics of the super hot plasma surrounding the black hole.  
    </p>
